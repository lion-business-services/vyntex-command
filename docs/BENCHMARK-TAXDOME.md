# Benchmark: TaxDome (inspiration only)

TaxDome is practice management software for accounting firms. This note records what its public material does well and how VYNTEX could apply the same principles in its own way.

## 2. What they do well

| Area | What TaxDome does | Principle | Original application for VYNTEX |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Packaging | Four product groups plus integrations ([home]); pages by firm size ([small], [mid]); each core term defined in one sentence ([workflow]). | Few groups, one vocabulary, a page per audience. | Group modules by the owner's day: win work, do the work, get paid, run the business. |
| Onboarding and demo | Three levels of commitment: tour, demo, sales ([home]). The [sales] page gives phone and email if the form fails. The [mid] page states 4 to 8 weeks of guided onboarding. The [help] center starts by role. | The visitor chooses the level of commitment and knows what follows the form. | Open demo as step one. Under "request a demo", state what happens next, how soon and in which language. |
| Pipelines and automation | A job is one paid piece of work moving through ordered stages ([h-workflow]). Actions run in order when it enters a stage, and it can advance by itself once the stage's open items are done ([h-auto]). Conditions use client tags ([h-cond]). Recurring jobs and templates exist ([h-pipe]). | One trigger an owner can hold in mind: "when a job gets here, do this; when it is done, move on". | Stage recipes per industry pack. Example: estimate accepted, so request the deposit, create crew tasks, ask the subcontractor for a W-9. Conditions on job facts (needs a permit). |
| Client portal | One place to upload, sign, pay, message and see a to-do list ([portal]). Requests can be answered without logging in ([requests]). Each client action advances the firm's work ([mobile]). | Every client action is a workflow event; remove login friction. | A per-job link sent by text: approve, sign, pay, see the visit date and photos. Label it with its real plan standing. |
| Documents and signatures | Folder templates, client upload, approval and built-in signing ([docs]). Identity-verified signing is paid per use; signing order, reminders and a completion certificate ([h-sign]). Documents can stay locked until the invoice is paid ([docs]). | Basic signing in the plan, extra verification metered; a signature unlocks the next step. | Contract, change order, lien waiver, W-9, insurance certificate. Signing on a phone at the job site. Expiry reminders. |
| Billing | Card and bank debit; the processing fee can appear to the client as its own line ([payments]). Deposit at proposal signing, final invoice collected automatically ([spring]). | Tie money to moments in the job. | Deposit, progress and final payments, then profit after crews, subcontractors and materials. Cash and checks as easy as cards. |
| Reporting | Widgets for jobs, tasks and pending client activity; team summary for owners and admins only ([dashboard]). Reports answer plain-language questions ([firm]). A forecast flags deadlines at risk ([spring]). | The first screen answers "what is waiting on whom". | Owner home on a phone: waiting on the client, the crew or me; money owed to me and by me. |
| Roles | Owner, admin, employee; rights grouped by area; staff see only assigned clients unless granted more; rights changes are logged ([h-roles]). | Least access by default, visibility by assignment, changes logged. | Crews see today's assigned jobs without prices. Subcontractors see their jobs and their own pay. |
| Pricing | Three plans per seat per year, from $800, $1,000 and $1,200 on a 1 year term, less on 2 and 3 year terms ([pricing], 2 Oct 2026). Monthly and four-month seasonal seats; a branded app as an add-on ([pricing]). Paid signature types drawn from a firm balance ([h-sign]). Custom quote from 25 seats ([quote]). | Four ideas kept apart: plan, add-on, pay as used, custom quote. Seats flex with the season. | Show the standings the product already uses as a legend on the pricing page. Seasonal crew access suits landscaping. |
| Trust | Rating and review count next to calls to action ([home]). The [trust] center lists audits, encryption and sign-in controls. The [ai] page says data is not used to train outside models and that actions wait for approval. | Trust claims are specific and sit next to the decision. | State only what is true today: who processes payments, how the assistant uses data, and that it asks before sending. |

A caution: the number of firms differs between [home] and [mid]. Keep every public figure in one source.

## 3. Where VYNTEX should deliberately differ

- **Phone first for the worker, not only the client.** Their firm side is built around desk work ([apps] includes a desktop drive); our users stand at job sites.
- **Spanish and English as equals**, including client-facing messages.
- **Usable in one sitting.** Multi-week onboarding ([mid]) does not fit a three-person crew.
- **Open demo first.** Their path leads to a sales conversation ([demo]).
- **Job profit, not billable hours**, with 1099 subcontractor compliance built in.
- **Fewer concepts.** Their model has many parts ([h-auto], [h-cond]); ours shows one idea, the job and its steps.
- **No long prepaid terms.** Their full seats are billed yearly upfront ([pricing]).

## 4. Top recommendations, by impact

1. Model automations as visible stage recipes that run in order when a job enters a stage and move it on when the required items are done. (larger)
2. Add a legend (included, add-on, pay as used, custom quote) to the pricing page, matching the badges in the product. (quick)
3. Under "request a demo", say what happens next, how soon and in which language, with phone or text as an alternative. (quick)
4. Give each job a no-password client link to approve, sign, pay and see the schedule, where each action advances the job. (larger)
5. Regroup the demo navigation and sales page into four groups that follow the owner's day. (quick)
6. Make the owner's home screen answer "waiting on whom" and "money in, money out" before any chart. (quick)
7. Tie payment requests to job moments (deposit, progress, final) and show profit per job. (larger)
8. Ship ready stage recipes in every industry pack so the demo opens with working automations. (quick)
9. Add subcontractor requests for W-9 and insurance with expiry reminders and year-end 1099 totals. (larger)
10. Publish a short trust page stating only what is true today, including how the assistant handles data and approvals. (quick)

## 5. Do not copy

Distinctive to TaxDome, and to stay theirs:

- Names of the company, its features, services, partners and plans, and its tax vocabulary.
- All wording: headlines, taglines, page text, help articles.
- Statistics, ratings, awards, testimonials and customer names.
- Page layouts, screenshots, illustrations, colours, the comparison table design and the savings calculator.
- Flows specific to tax work: client questionnaires, tax authority records, identity-question signing, return delivery.
- Their competitor comparison pages and their price points.
